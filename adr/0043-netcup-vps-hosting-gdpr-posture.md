# ADR-0043: KajianQ is hosted on a netcup GmbH VPS under a DPA, with a declared sub-processor register and fixed retention decisions

## Decision

1. **netcup GmbH (Germany/EU) is the host** for the API, the reverse proxy and Postgres with pgvector; the serving path that implements it is ADR-0044. The Cloudflare Workers and Neon path is superseded, not maintained in parallel.
2. **The DPA is concluded in the netcup customer control panel before any personal data lands on the box.** Concluding it is the owner's action, not an agent's.
3. **The sub-processor register lives in the Art. 30 record** (`docs/GDPR-ARTICLE-30-RECORD.md`), which is its source of truth, and the `/about` privacy notice renders its rows. The register rule: personal data never routes through a vendor's free tier — a vendor may carry personal data only on paid or standard terms under a data-processing agreement, which is what `personalDataAllowed` encodes and the provider seam enforces — and a row's tier change re-opens its verdict.
4. **Retention is decided here and valued in the Art. 30 record.** Anonymous sessions expire on inactivity, and the nightly reclamation removes expired sessions and the users they leave with no session, with their chat, traces and feedback. `DELETE /v1/auth/me` cascades the user's whole subtree. Access logs are bounded and rotated, and structured API logs carry a correlation id and never an address; the limiter's counters are in-memory only, named by a digest, and never written to a log. Backups are encrypted at rest, never a free tier, and a restore re-applies erasure, so a restored row cannot resurrect deleted data.
5. **A snapshot archive may carry personal data, and is flagged as such.** A snapshot is a whole-database dump, so the ObjectStore snapshot prefix is a personal-data-bearing location: its vendor joins the register, and an archive is encrypted at rest before personal data lands on the VPS. `verify`'s corpus-versus-ledger partition keeps its exact semantics — a red verify is never "fixed" by re-snapshotting without the personal tables, and a label is never deleted to reduce exposure. The newest verified snapshot per environment is retained, and a superseded one carrying personal data is deleted on a schedule.
6. **Nothing else changes.** Hosting changes where the data sits and who processes it; the session model (ADR-0017), the user-owned trace and its cascade erasure (ADR-0007), the store seams (ADR-0008) and engine purity are untouched.

## Why

netcup's contract notice makes a data-processing agreement legally obligatory when personal data is processed through its services, so the agreement precedes the data: hosting first would place chat content and logs under an agreement that does not exist yet. The household exception does not apply to a public service — KajianQ serves the public, so the operator is a controller processing through processors and an Art. 28(3) agreement is mandatory.

The register is a table rather than a paragraph because three audiences consume it: the notice renders its rows, the Art. 30 record derives its sub-processor chain from it, and the provider config's personal-data flag is its machine-checkable shadow. Stating retention as decisions is what makes the notice honest, and recording the snapshot flag is the safe option, because a whole-database dump has always carried chat and feedback rows and an unexamined archive on another vendor's storage is undeclared processing.

Rejected: self-hosting the API while the database stays with the vendor, which leaves the largest processing outside the agreement; keeping the previous host serving with the VPS used for batch jobs only — the one shape with no agreement obligation, but it buys less residency than it looks while adding a box to operate. Retained as an option, not a decision: a proxied DNS record in front of the box, which hides the origin without reducing what the host processes. Deferred: corpus-scoped dumps, which do not serve a transfer, and a managed EU Postgres, which restores a vendor and agreement chain without the operational savings.

## Consequences

- The register rule is enforced rather than asserted: every serving call site declares that it carries personal data, a compile error to drop, and the serving chains carry paid, personal-data-allowed heads.
- Capacity stops being a free plan's write ceiling; the corpus scope follows the store that holds it.
- Self-hosting is a single point of failure and an ops burden — TLS, log rotation, encrypted backups, restore drills and the nightly reclamation land on the operator — which is why this is a deploy-path change rather than an engine change.
- The snapshot tool reads a vendor-free connection variable, and its manifest records whether an archive carries personal data; creating a personal-data-bearing archive requires the target to be asserted encrypted at rest or the transitional exposure acknowledged.
- **Revisit when** a second region or a managed EU Postgres beats self-hosting on agreement and ops economics; snapshot archives become a real exposure (take the corpus-only projection); the host changes its sub-processor list; the product adopts real user identity; or a serving stage moves to a new vendor, or a vendor's tier changes.
