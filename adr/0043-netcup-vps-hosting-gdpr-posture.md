# ADR-0043: KajianQ is hosted on a netcup GmbH VPS under a DPA, with a declared sub-processor register and fixed retention values

## Decision

1. **netcup GmbH (Germany/EU) is the production host** for the API, the reverse proxy and Postgres + pgvector; the serving path that implements it is ADR-0044. The Cloudflare Workers + Neon path is superseded, not maintained in parallel.
2. **The DPA is concluded in the netcup CCP (Master Data → Order Processing) before any personal data lands on the box.** Concluding it is the owner's action, not an agent's.
3. **Sub-processor register** (the `/about` notice renders these rows; the register rule below is enforced at the seam):

   **The register rule:** personal data never routes through a vendor's **free tier** — only paid/standard terms with a DPA, which is what the `personalDataAllowed` column encodes and the provider factory enforces at the seam. The `Tier` column is a compliance field, not a cost note: changing a row's tier re-opens its verdict.

4. **Retention values** (the notice states these; the Art. 30 record carries them):
   - **Anonymous sessions: 30 days of inactivity** — only the token's SHA-256 hash is stored, expiry is enforced on read, and the nightly reclamation removes expired sessions and the users they leave with no session, whose chat, traces and feedback the FK cascade takes with them. There is no separate age-based deletion of chat rows.
   - **Erasure on demand: `DELETE /v1/auth/me`** cascades the whole subtree — the Art. 17 path.
   - **Server access logs: 14 days**, rotated daily and size-capped; API structured logs carry a correlation id and no IP address, and rate-limit counters are transient runtime state, never written to a log file.
   - **Backups: encrypted at rest, 30-day rolling retention** — key outside the repo, rotated daily, never a free tier; a restore re-applies erasure, so a restored row cannot resurrect deleted data.
5. **A snapshot archive may carry personal data, and is flagged as such.** A snapshot is a whole-database dump, so the ObjectStore snapshot prefix is a personal-data-bearing location: its vendor joins the register, and an archive is encrypted at rest before personal data lands on the VPS. `verify`'s corpus-vs-ledger partition keeps its exact semantics — a red verify is never "fixed" by re-snapshotting without the personal tables, and a label is never deleted to reduce exposure. The newest verified snapshot per environment is retained; a superseded one carrying personal data is deleted 30 days after its successor verified.
6. **Nothing else changes.** Hosting changes where the data sits and who processes it; the session model (ADR-0017), the user-owned trace and its cascade erasure (ADR-0007), the store seams (ADR-0008) and engine purity are untouched.

## Why

netcup's contract notice makes a DPA legally obligatory when personal data is processed through its services and assumes no personal-data processing without one — so the agreement precedes the data, and the ticket graph's ordering is the legal ordering, not a schedule.

- **The household exception does not apply to a public service** — KajianQ serves the public, so the operator is a controller (Art. 4(7)) processing through processors (Art. 4(8)) and an Art. 28(3) DPA is mandatory; even where the exception were arguable, the migration's own gate is stricter than the law's floor.
- **A DPA is a precondition, not paperwork to follow the move** — hosting first would place chat content and logs under an agreement that does not exist yet.
- **The register is a table, not a paragraph, because three audiences consume it**: the notice renders its rows, the Art. 30 record derives its sub-processor chain from it, and `models.json`'s `personalDataAllowed` is its machine-checkable shadow.
- **Fixing retention as numbers is what makes the notice honest** — Art. 13(1)(e) requires telling data subjects how long their data is kept.
- **The snapshot flag is written down because silence would be the dangerous option** — a whole-DB dump has always carried chat and feedback rows, and an unexamined archive on another vendor's storage is undeclared processing.
- Rejected: self-hosting the API while the database stays on the vendor — the database holds the personal data, so the largest processing would stay outside the DPA. Rejected too: keeping Cloudflare serving with the VPS used only for the ingest and eval CLIs — the one shape with no DPA obligation, but it buys less residency than it looks while adding a box to operate.
- Retained as an option, not a decision: a proxied DNS record in front of the box — it hides the origin and absorbs volume, without reducing what netcup processes. Deferred: corpus-scoped dumps (they do not serve the transfer) and a managed EU Postgres (it restores a vendor + DPA chain without the operational savings).

## Consequences

- **The register rule is enforced, not asserted**: every serving call site declares `personalData: true` as a required, literal-true field on its seam type — dropping it is a compile error — the serving chains carry paid, personal-data-allowed heads, and `apps/api/src/lib/personal-data-serving.test.ts` fails if a serving role loses its keyed personal-data-allowed candidate or a free-tier vendor returns to a chain head.
- **The corpus no longer depends on a free plan's write ceiling**: staging's "smallest corpus that satisfies the gate" was shaped by it, and on the VPS that ceiling becomes the disk — ADR-0039's scoping decision is untouched.
- **Self-hosting is a single point of failure and an ops burden** — TLS, logrotate, encrypted backups, restore drills and the nightly reclamation land on the operator, which is why this is a deploy-path change, not an engine change.
- **`db:snapshot` reads a vendor-free connection env**, and its manifest records whether an archive carries personal data: `create` refuses to write a personal-data-bearing archive unless the target is asserted encrypted at rest or the transitional exposure is acknowledged.
- **Revisit when** a second region or a managed EU Postgres beats self-hosting on DPA and ops economics; snapshot archives become a real exposure (take the corpus-only projection); netcup changes its sub-processor list; the product adopts real user identity; or any serving stage moves to a new vendor, or a `models.json` tier changes.
- **The live stack statement lives in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §15/§8 and `SPECS.md` §3.2/§5**, not here; `packages/infra/scripts/db-snapshot.mjs` owns snapshot creation and the corpus-vs-ledger partition, and `apps/web/src/lib/privacy-notice.ts` renders the register and the retention values into the notice; the executed migration is GDPR-E (#181), recorded with its evidence in `docs/VPS-CUTOVER-RECORD.md`; and `CONTEXT.md` is unchanged, because the register and the retention windows are compliance artifacts, not product concepts.
